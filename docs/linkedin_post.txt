🔊 A faulty bearing sounds different. But can a machine hear the difference before a human notices? Yes — here's how.

POST:
Noise, Vibration, and Harshness (NVH) quality issues cost automotive OEMs billions annually in warranty claims and recalls. Catching drivetrain faults early — before they cascade into expensive failures — is the goal of Condition-Based Monitoring (CBM).

In this project, I built a 4-class drivetrain fault classifier:
🔴 Outer-race bearing fault (periodic impulses at bearing defect frequency)
🟠 Gear tooth defect (amplitude modulation at mesh frequency)
🟡 Mass imbalance (sinusoidal at 1× shaft)
🟢 Healthy baseline

Method:
→ Simulated vibration signals at 12.8 kHz sampling rate
→ FFT-based power in 8 frequency bands + statistical features (RMS, Crest Factor, Peak)
→ PCA for visualization — classes form clearly separable clusters
→ Random Forest classifier

Results: Perfect 4-class separation — the frequency bands are highly discriminative for these physics-based signals.

This methodology directly applies to end-of-line (EOL) quality testing at OEM assembly plants.
